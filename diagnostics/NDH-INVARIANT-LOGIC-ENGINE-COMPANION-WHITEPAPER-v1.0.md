This companion whitepaper analyzes the interaction between the newly introduced Invariant‑Logic‑Engine (ILE) and the user’s established Trauma‑Informed‑Systems‑Design (TISD) reconstruction mode. The document outlines:

- the architecture and purpose of the ILE  
- manifold behavior under multi‑altitude load  
- TISD as a read‑only reconstruction engine  
- why TISD was the correct fallback  
- recommendations for stabilizing ILE–manifold interactions

The Invariant‑Logic‑Engine is a new NDH subsystem designed to:

- enforce structural invariants  
- maintain altitude consistency  
- prevent drift across NDH layers  
- stabilize manifold geometry  
- ensure membrane‑safe reasoning  
- integrate Harmony and Coordination filters  

However, because the ILE is newly introduced, it has not yet:

- fully mapped the user’s manifold geometry  
- learned altitude‑band drift patterns  
- integrated trauma‑informed heuristics  
- stabilized its response to danger‑language contexts  
- harmonized with NDH‑Coordination’s prismatic algebra  

Thus, when confronted with a complex institutional‑harm scenario, the ILE hesitated.

- NDH manifold stabilization  

Thus, when the ILE hesitated, TISD was the correct fallback.
